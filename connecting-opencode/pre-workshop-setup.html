Getting your laptop ready

A one-time setup so you can start building on Day 2 instead of installing. Written step by step, with pictures of every screen, for people who have never done anything like this before.

Time needed — about 20 minutes Cost — nothing, no card needed Works on — Windows and Mac

Please read this first

Please complete this before the workshop. We start building on Day 2, and this setup needs to be done on your own laptop beforehand. It takes about twenty minutes and you only ever do it once.

If you get stuck, don't worry and don't give up. Stop where you are, and bring your laptop to the session — tell us what you saw and we'll sort it out together in a few minutes. Getting stuck is common and helping you is part of the plan, not an inconvenience.

You cannot break anything. Everything here is free, safe, and easy to remove afterwards. Nobody is going to see your screen or your files.

Do not worry if the words are unfamiliar. You are only copying a few lines of text and clicking a few buttons. Understanding what they mean is what the workshop is for.

Before you start

Three things to have ready.

  • Your laptop — Windows or Mac, old or new, both are fine. Please bring this same laptop to the workshop.
  • A Google account you can sign into. Any Gmail address works.
  • An internet connection for about twenty minutes.

The six steps

Do them in order, and check each one before moving on to the next. Steps 4 and 5 have a picture of every screen you'll see.

Step 1 Open the terminal 1 minute

The terminal is a plain window where you type instructions instead of clicking icons. It is completely safe — it's just a different door into the same computer.

Windows
Press the Windows key Type: powershell Press Enter
Mac
Press Cmd + Space Type: terminal Press Enter
You should see A window with some text and a blinking cursor. Keep this window open — you'll need it for the steps below.
One thing to know

Where this guide shows a line starting with >, that symbol just marks the start of a command. Don't type the > itself — only what comes after it.

Step 2 Install Node.js 5 minutes

Node.js is the engine our tool runs on. You install it once and never think about it again.

  1. Go to nodejs.org in your web browser.
  2. Click the download button marked LTS. That stands for Long Term Support — it's the stable version. If you see two buttons, LTS is the one you want.
  3. Open the file you downloaded and click through the installer, accepting every default.
Windows
You'll download a .msi file Double-click it Click Next until it finishes
Mac
You'll download a .pkg file Double-click it Click Continue until it finishes
Note There is nothing to choose or configure. Accept every default the installer offers.
Step 3 Check Node, then install OpenCode 4 minutes

First, close your terminal window completely and open a new one. A terminal opened before the install won't know Node exists yet — this trips up almost everybody, so please don't skip it.

Now type this and press Enter:

> node -v
You should see A version number such as v20.11.0. Yours may be different — that's fine. Seeing a number rather than an error is the whole test.

If you saw a number, install the agent with this command. It's identical on Windows and Mac:

> npm install -g opencode-ai
You should see Text scrolling for up to a minute, ending with a summary. Yellow warning text is completely normal — only red errors matter.

Check it worked:

> opencode --version
You should see Another version number. If so, the hardest part is behind you.
Step 4 Get your free Google AI key 5 minutes

This part happens in your web browser, not the terminal. The key is what lets the tool talk to Google's AI. Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey and sign in with your Google account.

To get the full key, click on the key in that list. A details window opens.

Two things worth knowing

It's free — you will not be asked for a credit card at any point. And treat it like a password: it's yours alone, so don't share or post it. If you ever lose it, you just create another.

While you're there, please create a second key by clicking Create API key once more. Free keys have daily limits, and having a spare turns a frustrating moment during the workshop into a ten-second fix. Save both.

Step 5 Connect your key to OpenCode 4 minutes

Back in the terminal. First make a folder to work in and go into it — these two commands are the same on Windows and Mac:

> mkdir my-agent > cd my-agent

Now start the agent by typing:

> opencode

Once it has started, type /connect. You don't even need to finish the word — a menu appears as soon as you start typing.

A list of AI providers opens. This is the one place people go wrong, so read the next picture carefully.

Careful — the easy mistake

The very first option, OpenCode Zen, is labelled “(Recommended)”. That is not the one we want. It's a paid service, and it's only labelled that way because it's the tool-maker's own product.

For this workshop, always scroll down and pick Google — that's the free one your key belongs to.

OpenCode now asks for your key. Paste it in with Ctrl + V on Windows or Cmd + V on Mac, then press Enter.

If it worked, you'll be returned to the main screen — and the important change is at the bottom of the input box.

Can't see it? If the status line doesn't mention Google, your key probably wasn't accepted. Type /connect and try again — see the troubleshooting table below.
Step 6 The final test 1 minute

Let's make sure it can actually do something. Type this where it says “Ask anything…” and press Enter:

> create a file called hello.txt containing a friendly greeting for a teacher
You should see The agent thinking for a moment, then telling you it created the file. That's it — you're ready for the workshop.

You're done if all of these are true

  • node -v shows a version number.
  • opencode --version shows a version number.
  • You have your Google AI key saved somewhere safe — ideally two of them.
  • The status line at the bottom of OpenCode mentions Google.
  • The agent created a hello.txt file when you asked it to.
If that all worked

You've just installed and used an AI coding agent — the thing the whole workshop is about. Nothing else is needed beforehand. Please leave everything installed exactly as it is, and bring this same laptop with you.

If something went wrong

These are the common ones and they're all fixable. Try the fix once — and if it still doesn't work, stop there and bring it to the session.

What you sawWhat it meansTry this
'node' is not recognized Your terminal was open before you installed Node. Close the terminal completely, open a brand new one, and try again. This fixes it most of the time.
command not found: node The Mac wording of the same thing. Close Terminal, reopen it, try again.
'opencode' is not recognized The install didn't finish, or the terminal is stale. Open a new terminal and run the npm install -g opencode-ai command again.
The status line doesn't say Google The key wasn't accepted, or a different provider was chosen by mistake. Type /connect, choose Google (not OpenCode Zen), and paste the key again.
401 / invalid API key The key was pasted with a character missing or an extra space. Go back to the key details window and use the Copy key button, then /connect and paste again.
429 / rate limit / quota You asked the free service for too much too quickly. Nothing is broken. Wait a minute and try once more, or connect your second key.
Permission denied You're in a folder that doesn't allow changes. Close the terminal, open a new one, and run the Step 5 commands again from the start.
Lots of yellow text during install Ordinary warnings, not errors. Nothing to do. Carry on to the next step.
Genuinely — don't struggle alone

If you've tried the fix and it still isn't working, please stop. Nothing is lost and you haven't fallen behind. Come to the session, tell us what you saw, and we'll sort it out together in a few minutes. Every single problem on this page has a straightforward solution when someone can look at your screen.

If it helps, take a photo of the message on your screen and bring it with you.

Questions people usually ask

Is any of this going to cost money?

No. Node.js and OpenCode are free and open source. The Google AI key uses a free allowance that doesn't ask for a credit card — you can see this yourself in picture 1, where the billing tier reads “Free tier”. The whole workshop is deliberately built on tools that cost nothing, so you can keep using them afterwards and hand them to your students.

Will this slow down or damage my laptop?

No. These are small programs, similar in size to an ordinary app. Nothing runs in the background, and everything can be uninstalled normally afterwards if you'd like.

Do my files get sent to Google?

Your files stay on your laptop. When you ask the agent a question, the relevant piece of context travels to the AI so it can answer — the same as pasting something into ChatGPT. Nothing is uploaded wholesale, and nothing happens without you asking.

What is “Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite”?

That's simply which of Google's AI models you're connected to — you saw it in picture 6. OpenCode picks a sensible free one for you automatically. You don't need to change it, and we'll talk about what the different models mean during the workshop.

I've never used a terminal. Am I going to be behind?

Not at all, and you certainly won't be alone. A good number of people coming have never opened one either — which is exactly why Day 2 starts by teaching it slowly and from the beginning. This guide is a head start, not a test.

What if my school laptop won't let me install things?

That happens, and it isn't your fault. Bring the laptop anyway and let us know — there are ways around it, and it's much easier to solve with someone next to you.
